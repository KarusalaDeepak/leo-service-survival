# Data provenance and evidence boundaries

## WetLinks

Primary empirical source: D. Laniewski, E. Lanfer, B. Meijerink, R. van Rijswijk-Deij, and N. Aschenbruck, “WetLinks: A Large-Scale Longitudinal Starlink Dataset with Contiguous Weather Data,” TMA 2024, DOI: 10.23919/TMA62044.2024.10558998.

The two supplied analysis tables contain longitudinal end-to-end RTT, loss, throughput, and local weather observations at Enschede and Osnabrück. They are nearby temperate-European locations. They do not provide serving-satellite, beam, gateway, handover, physical attenuation, or root-cause labels. The source project identifies CC BY-SA 4.0 terms.

## Palo Alto Starlink ping record

Exploratory external transport-stress source: L. Borgianni, “11-Day Starlink Ping Measurements Dataset (Palo Alto, US, May 2024),” Zenodo, version 1, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.14987305. The record is a supplement to L. Borgianni, D. Adami, S. Giordano, and C. Chafe, “Global Net Equity: The Role of LEO Satellites Towards 6G Connectivity Landscape,” IEEE MeditCom 2025, DOI: 10.1109/MeditCom64437.2025.11104460.

The 12 daily files cover May 9–20, 2024. The source describes bursts of ten ICMP packets every ten seconds from a Starlink-connected client at Stanford University/Palo Alto to a terrestrial server, with a ground station and point of presence identified in San Jose. The runner aggregates these summaries into five-minute bins, applies the same RTT/loss service-usability endpoint, and generates predictions from models already fitted and, where applicable, calibrated only on WetLinks. No Palo Alto value is used for refitting, recalibration, tuning, or model selection.

This record contains RTT and packet-loss summaries but no throughput, weather, serving-satellite, beam, or stated timestamp-time-zone field. The unavailable WetLinks covariates, including cyclical clock-time fields, remain missing and covariate-based models replace them with medians learned from the WetLinks training partition. It is one short vantage point and exhibits a strong endpoint/event-rate shift, so its metrics are descriptive transport-stress evidence—not confirmatory external validation and not evidence of model superiority or physical cause. The Zenodo record page did not expose a license value when this provenance note was prepared; authors must verify redistribution terms before publication.

## COMMECT

The processed concurrent 5G/Starlink trace comes from the acquisition documented as “Radio KPI & Latency Measurement of Cellular and Satellite Networks for Evaluating Multi-Connectivity Solutions in Livestock Transport Monitoring in Rural Areas,” Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.14620779. The public Zenodo landing page did not expose a license value when this provenance note was prepared; authors must inspect any terms included in the downloaded archive and verify redistribution permission before publication.

Only one acquisition campaign is available. It supports purged offline shadow
replay, not independent deployment validation. Two derived protocols must be
identified separately: `P4-COMMECT-5S-CS` uses a five-second all-five-future-samples
endpoint and a 1,225-second conservative scheduled denominator, whereas
`P2-COMMECT-1S-CC-FTR` uses a fixed one-second next-interval endpoint and 812 complete
cases from 948 scheduled epochs. The latter's filter--then--rank rule is an
ungated ablation outside its declared candidate family. These protocols differ
in split, horizon, completeness rule, policy, and denominator; neither is a live
installed-action experiment, and their percentages are not replications.

## LENS-derived artifact

The local table is a small released prediction artifact derived upstream from LENS-related work, not the raw measurement archive. Consecutive landmarks overlap by as many as 30 future bins, and the sessions are too short for a purged three-way survival evaluation. The runner therefore exports counts and a diagnostic reason but no LENS survival score. The upstream provenance record refers to DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.15331299; authors must verify the full provenance chain and redistribution terms.

## Paper 1 geometry table

`regional_starlink_risk_aware_visibility.csv` is derived from the Paper 1 workflow. TLE/SGP4 information contributes deterministic orbital geometry only. The table's engineering survival/drop quantities are model outputs under selected assumptions; they are not observations of RF loss or network outage. The run manifest records the exact table hash. ## Trace-driven scheduler

Measured WetLinks episodes supply service outcomes and predictions. Task releases, deadlines, priorities, users, satellite identities, ground stations, and resource conflicts are generated by the controlled benchmark. Its results are conditional stress-test outcomes, not operational Earth-observation evidence.

## Evidence-tier vocabulary for the additional sources

The additional datasets and simulator runs use three distinct evidence labels.
They must not be pooled under a generic claim of “real-world validation.”

- `field_observational` means that the retained network or terminal values were
  measured in a field trace. It does not mean randomized deployment, causal
  policy evaluation, statistical independence of high-rate rows, or observed
  Earth-observation completion.
- `measured_trace_replay` means that an endpoint or decision experiment was
  replayed over measured trace values. The resulting completion proxy is
  derived and is not an operational application acknowledgement.
- `synthetic_simulation` means that the scenario, network behavior, outcome, or
  some combination of them was generated by a benchmark or simulator. It is
  useful for controlled feasibility and stress testing but is not operator or
  field evidence.

The canonical public-trace audit is
`results/public_trace_validation_v3/RESULTS_CLAIMS_LEDGER.md`; its file hashes
are fixed in `results/public_trace_validation_v3/SHA256SUMS`. Earlier
`public_trace_validation` directories are retained history and are not the
canonical source for paper numbers.

## NUWiNS concurrent Starlink and cellular road tests

The NUWiNS SIGMETRICS 2026 release contains Alaska June 2024, Hawaii August
2024, and Los-Angeles-to-Omaha November 2024 road-test campaigns spanning
Starlink and AT&T, T-Mobile, and Verizon. The Alaska and Hawaii MPTCP/MPShell
panels are publisher-aligned two-path observations. Their RTT and throughput
panels remain separate because the release does not provide a defensible key
for silently fusing them. The mainland files are independent per-operator
traces; any nearest-time pairing must use a declared tolerance, retain both
source times and skew, and fail closed when the tolerance is not met.

Raw normalized QoS rows are `field_observational`. The frozen 5 MB
Alaska-to-Hawaii future-capacity experiment is `measured_trace_replay`; it is a
fixed-payload proxy, not an observed EO task, path-selection trial, or proof of
consistent model superiority. Although the release includes cellular mobility
events and Starlink outage/switch fields, it does not provide serving-satellite,
beam, gateway, orbital-geometry, scheduler-action, EO-workload, or EO-completion
records.

The dataset is CC BY 4.0 and the accompanying code is MIT. Redistribution must
retain attribution and the CC BY license notice and indicate modifications.
The acquisition record and verified archive hashes are in
`data/external/nuwins_sigmetrics26/LOCAL_ACQUISITION_RECORD.md`. Canonical
summaries, replay metrics, uncertainty intervals, source identifiers, and claim
limits are under `results/public_trace_validation_v3/`.

## Four-site LENS Starlink subset

The bounded LENS subset uses Starlink probes and dish status from Brisbane,
Ghana, Seattle, and Victoria. These are four sites for one access operator, not
four independent operators. Probe-to-dish joins use causal backward alignment;
the retained 100% alignment fraction results from selecting bounded probe
members inside the telemetry intervals and must not be reported as reliability
or availability. LENS does not provide a concurrent 5G path, serving-satellite,
beam, gateway, scheduler action, operational EO task, or completion
acknowledgement for these samples.

The measurement portal states CC BY-SA 4.0; attributed redistributed
derivatives must preserve the share-alike conditions. The LENS code repository
contains a GNU GPL v3 license, which is distinct from the measurement-data
license. Public ping files can expose network addresses, so publication should
prefer bounded aggregates and source hashes rather than republishing raw
address-bearing payloads. The repository and Victoria acquisition details are
in `data/external/lens/LOCAL_ACQUISITION_RECORD.md`; the four-site canonical
coverage, QoS, and alignment outputs are
`results/public_trace_validation_v3/campaign_site_operator_coverage.csv`,
`results/public_trace_validation_v3/path_qos_distributions.csv`, and
`results/public_trace_validation_v3/lens_telemetry_alignment.csv`.

This four-site raw-measurement analysis is separate from the earlier
“LENS-derived artifact” described above; the limitations of that small upstream
prediction table must not be transferred to, or confused with, the acquired
probe/dish archives.

## LENS OneWeb Iowa assignment and RTT subset

The official LENS OneWeb 2024 archive supplies AIM terminal tracking
assignments, source-reported `AVERAGE_SINR`, ping replies, and a contemporaneous
TLE snapshot for one terminal in Ames, Iowa. The bounded canonical interval
contains an AIM satellite/track assignment and two adjacent assignment changes.
Nearest successful-reply joins use a frozen 50 ms tolerance and retain rejected
candidates for audit. These are `field_observational` assignment transitions,
not operator-confirmed protocol handovers or independent row-level experimental
units. The zero-padded AIM satellite-to-TLE association is a deterministic name
match, not a jointly logged operator identity assertion.

The Zenodo record and LENS documentation specify CC BY-SA 4.0. Any
redistributed derivative must retain attribution and share-alike terms. The
verified source and exact limitations are recorded in
`data/external/lens_oneweb_iowa_2024/README.md` and
`data/external/lens_oneweb_iowa_2024/RESULTS_CLAIMS_LEDGER.md`. Canonical
normalized panels and manifests are under
`data/external/lens_oneweb_iowa_2024/canonical/`; the cross-source coverage
audit is under `results/matched_telemetry_audit_v1/`.

This source contributes observed terminal assignment, RF/pointing, and RTT
context for one OneWeb site. It provides neither beam nor physical gateway
identity and contains no policy action, EO workload, application completion, or
baseline comparison. It therefore cannot complete the end-to-end framework on
its own.

## SatInView inferred serving-satellite artifact

The SatInView CSV contains ping RTT and post-processed trajectory/obstruction-
map matches. Its `Connected_Satellite`, elevation, azimuth, and distance fields
are inferred, not operator-confirmed control-plane telemetry. Consecutive label
changes may be reported only as inferred identity changes; they are not direct
handover observations. The artifact lacks beam, gateway, RF, scheduler-action,
EO-workload, and application-completion fields, and its withheld site geometry
prevents exact independent reconstruction.

No explicit redistribution license was present in the acquired repository
snapshot. Keep the repository and row-level derivatives private unless the
authors clarify terms; citation alone is not a redistribution grant. The
source record is `data/external/sat_in_view/LOCAL_ACQUISITION_RECORD.md`.
Permitted private aggregate outputs are
`results/public_trace_validation_v3/sat_in_view_inference_summary.csv` and the
descriptive coverage audit under `results/matched_telemetry_audit_v1/`. Both
are `field_observational` with the inferred-identity qualification.

## NEXASPHERE Athens Starlink archive

The Athens archive supplies public-target latency/loss summaries, Starlink dish
status, and periodic throughput tests for one site. The adapter restricts all
tables to the metadata-declared interval
`2026-05-11T12:06:16Z`--`2026-05-18T12:07:03Z`; an April pilot present in the
CSV files is excluded. The tables are `field_observational`, but they are
separately sampled observations, not a synchronized alternative-path policy
trial or an operational availability experiment. They contain no concurrent
terrestrial path, serving-satellite, beam, gateway, continuous RF, policy
action, EO workload, or completion acknowledgement.

The locally inspected record did not establish sufficiently clear reuse terms.
Until the license is resolved, use is restricted to private analysis: do not
redistribute raw or row-level data, and do not place even the private aggregate
in a redistributed manuscript without resolving the reuse terms. Acquisition
integrity and the frozen window are recorded in
`data/external/nexasphere_athens_2026/LOCAL_ACQUISITION_RECORD.md`. The only
canonical analysis artifact is the restricted aggregate
`results/public_trace_validation_v3/nexasphere_athens_private_summary.csv`,
with its restriction also recorded in the v3 manifest and claims ledger.

## FjordLink maritime Starlink and 5G campaign

FjordLink is a four-month Kiel Fjord campaign containing Starlink terminal
statistics, 5G-router measurements, TWAMP latency/jitter, iPerf3 throughput,
and weather context. The source is released under CC BY 4.0. The frozen source
commit, license hashes, and primary client archive hash are recorded in
`data/external/fjordlink/LOCAL_ACQUISITION_RECORD.md`.

The local adapter parses operator-specific TWAMP sessions directly from the
released ZIP archive and retains logger timestamps on their source clock; the
release does not declare a timezone, so they are not silently relabeled as
UTC. It then performs an explicit interval-overlap audit between Starlink and
terrestrial sessions. Only sessions satisfying the frozen minimum source-clock
overlap may be called synchronized multi-access evidence. A combined-dataset
description, nearby dates, different sessions, or common collection hardware
are not sufficient. Rejected sessions remain useful `field_observational` QoS
and distribution-shift evidence.

FjordLink has no EO policy actions or completion acknowledgements. It cannot
be described as an operational path-selection trial or EO workload validation,
and separate rows, sessions, or days cannot be inflated into independent
campaigns.

## EOS-Bench and SPOT5 scheduling benchmarks

EOS-Bench scenarios are generated with Orekit. The acquired city-target files
use realistic target locations, but their missions, opportunities, resources,
and schedules are simulator-generated. The integrated EOS run is therefore
`synthetic_simulation`. The canonical integrated output is
`results/eos_full_chain_synthetic_v2/`, whose `run_manifest.json` identifies
the exact EOS-Bench scenario, hashes, seeds, simulated network overlay, and the
claim boundary “synthetic simulation, not operational or field evidence.” It
must not be described as actual EO completion or validation of network
reliability. Canonical acquired scenarios are under
`data/external/eos_bench/extracted/`.

No explicit license was found in the acquired EOS-Bench Git revision or
Hugging Face dataset metadata. `CITATION.cff` is citation metadata, not a reuse
grant. Treat the acquired code, scenarios, and derived copies as private and do
not redistribute them without author permission. Aggregate facts needed to
identify a private experiment should preserve source attribution and hashes.

SPOT5's 21 daily photograph-scheduling instances were produced by a simulator
of a future order book. They are also `synthetic_simulation`, not raw
operational orders, flight telemetry, executed schedules, or communications
measurements. Mendeley Data declares CC BY 4.0; redistribution requires
attribution, a license link, and an indication of modifications. Canonical
sources are under `data/external/spot5/extracted/SPOT5 benchmarks/`. No SPOT5
result directory is currently designated canonical; do not imply that the EOS
v2 run used SPOT5. The complete acquisition, parsing, licensing, and claim
audit for both benchmark families is `data/external/ACQUISITION_AUDIT.md`.

These benchmark families support scheduling feasibility, constraint,
scalability, and compatibility tests. Any latency, outage, congestion,
handover, or completion behavior overlaid on them must remain explicitly
simulated and cannot be promoted to field evidence.

## ns-3 and 5G-LENA NTN simulations

Both ns-3 tiers are `synthetic_simulation` evidence. The stock ns-3.48 plus
5G-LENA tier is a packet-level geometry/channel/connectivity smoke test; its
stock example does not model satellite propagation delay. The UW FUNLAB
ns-3-NTN tier models slant-range delay and transparent/regenerative NTN timing,
but the validated runs are short, one-satellite smoke experiments. Neither
tier supplies operator-proprietary constellation, beam, gateway, handover, or
scheduler fidelity, and neither is Starlink/OneWeb performance or operational
EO completion evidence. No network-profile CSV was created because those runs
cannot estimate rate, outage, and handover distributions without inventing
fields.

Pinned source commits, the scoped stock-coordinate correction, build host, and
license boundary are recorded in `simulation/ns3_5g_lena/PROVENANCE.md`.
Canonical stock evidence is
`simulation/ns3_5g_lena/results/stock_vsat_realistic_final/`; canonical UW NTN
evidence is `simulation/ns3_ntn/results/final/`, including
`simulation/ns3_ntn/results/final/summary.json`. Validated commands and the
permitted interpretation are in
`simulation/ns3_5g_lena/VALIDATION_RESULTS.md`.

The ns-3 trees state that contributions are predominantly GPL-2.0-only, with
component-specific license files; the pinned UW tree is GPL-2.0-only, and the
NR components must remain license-compatible. Before conveying modified source
or a source-containing bundle, preserve notices and satisfy the applicable
GPL/source-availability obligations for every included component. Simulator
licensing does not change the evidence tier of generated traces: they remain
synthetic results rather than field measurements.

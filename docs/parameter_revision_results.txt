PAPER 4 R5 — COEFFICIENT AND PARAMETER REVISION
5 November 2026

What was implemented

1. The full-chain prediction formula no longer uses the hand-written constants
   0.98, 0.99, 0.65, 4, 0.85 and 250. Logistic coefficients are fitted on 6,000
   generated development examples. Separate 3,000-example partitions tune
   regularization, calibrate probabilities and check those probabilities.
   Evaluation uses disjoint seeds. A wider development-only regularization
   audit still selects C=0.01, now inside the expanded range.

2. The endpoint audit rebuilds 15 profiles and records the unsupported one-minute
   profile. It varies latency, loss, persistence, horizon and supported bin sizes.
   One-minute measurements are not invented. Changing the gap parameter has no
   effect because an unobserved bin already censors the episode; that is a
   property of this implementation, not evidence that missing service was good.

3. Two nested chronological development folds compare original and wider search
   ranges. Mean IBS is 0.041444 for the original grid and 0.041549 for the wider
   grid. Lower is better: the wider grid does not improve this descriptive result.
   Some choices remain at search boundaries, so no universal optimum is claimed.

4. Calibration-support limits, probability clipping, total-hazard bounds and
   feature-domain budgets are tested explicitly. Hazard bounds do not activate
   on the audited development rows. A separate correctness fix makes the active
   cause-incidence probabilities agree with the same bounded total used by
   survival. Earlier measured results are preserved rather than relabelled.

These conditions reuse one EOS-Bench geometry and ten generated seeds. Two
neutral-condition seeds favour the baseline and one ties. The predictor stays
fixed across conditions, but the domain-gate reference is condition-specific.
The intervals are not simultaneous independent-campaign bounds. These results
do not establish field benefit or a causal benefit from calibration alone.

The new endpoint comparison is also mixed. Under the primary profile the audit
learner's IBS is 0.077581 versus 0.077983 for Kaplan–Meier. At a 70-ms latency
limit the values are 0.102512 versus 0.099682. The simpler model is better in
that second comparison. Different service definitions can change the ordering.

What is deliberately not claimed

The original Paper 1 geometry, beam and atmospheric coefficients have not been
estimated from matched physical observations. This revision addresses learned
service-level prediction, numerical transparency and controlled robustness;
it does not validate those physical mechanisms. Application requirements and
risk budgets remain declared choices, not coefficients that should be learned
by maximizing test performance. The measured analyses remain retrospective.
Live hardware execution remains NOT_RUN; no new GitHub push was performed.
